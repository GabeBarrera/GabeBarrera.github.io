About

I'm an Information Security Analyst II at the Port of San Diego. I hunt threats, harden identity, and automate the busywork out of incident response.

I studied computer science at San Diego State University and joined the Port as a software developer intern in 2018. That's where I helped the incident response team bring systems back after a company-wide ransomware attack, and it's why I moved to security in 2019.

Most days I'm in Microsoft Defender XDR and Sentinel, Palo Alto traffic and InfoBlox logs, hunting for anything that looks like compromise, then containing and cleaning it up within SLA. I write KQL detections from research on new threats and critical vulnerabilities. Before we had a SIEM, I built a central event collection and alerting system in Azure that cut our time to detect by about 70% and our time to respond by about 30%.

On the identity side, I rolled out Okta MFA and certificate-based authentication, audit Active Directory access, and harden machines to CIS benchmarks. I automate what I can with PowerShell, Logic Apps and the Graph API. I also review third-party SOC 2 and ISO 27001 reports, assess risk on new apps and vulnerabilities, and take apart suspicious executables in Ghidra.

Off the clock it's the same habit in a different shape: a Raspberry Pi packet lab, an offline AI assistant, and games where defenders get the good tools.

Projects

Things I've built to learn how systems behave. Each one taught me something I couldn't have read.

  • Sentinel Unified System

    One pipeline for security events at work: custom KQL analytics rules in Sentinel feed Logic Apps that open and update SolarWinds Service Desk tickets and post to Teams, so every detection is tracked from alert to close.

    In production
    SentinelKQLLogic Apps

  • The Hunt

    An investigation into a highly suspicious user. I built detection alerts around the account, learned to read and write Russian to follow the trail, and traced the chain of command to settle whether the account was legitimate.

    Closed
    Threat huntingOSINT

  • Pi Packet Lab

    A Raspberry Pi with two Wi-Fi adapters: one keeps the management link up while an ALFA adapter sits in monitor mode capturing frames. Captures go into Wireshark to baseline my own network and spot devices that talk more than they should.

    Ongoing
    WiresharkKali802.11

  • Offline Assistant

    A local language model on a Raspberry Pi 5 that runs its own Wi-Fi access point and serves a plain HTML chat page. Nothing leaves the device, so it works in a cabin, on a plane, or on a network you don't trust.

    Building
    Pi 5Local LLMhostapd

  • The Heist

    A capture-the-flag game set inside a company building. Attackers work to lift the flag while defenders patrol, patch, and watch logs, all within a 30-minute in-game workday. It's built to make blue-team play feel as tense as red.

    In development
    Game designRed vs blue

  • Slow Screen

    A Raspberry Pi 3B+ driving a Waveshare e-Paper display over GPIO. It draws almost no power and only changes when something worth seeing changes, which suits a status board you glance at, not stare at.
